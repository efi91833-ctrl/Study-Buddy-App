One heartbeat, step by step no background needed

The heart is two pumps side by side. The right side sends used, low-oxygen blood to the lungs; the left side sends fresh, oxygen-rich blood to the body. Each side has a small top chamber (atrium, a waiting room) and a big bottom chamber (ventricle, the pump). Four one-way doors, the valves, keep blood moving forward.

The one rule to rememberBlood only moves from higher pressure to lower pressure. A valve is pushed open when the pressure behind it is higher than in front of it, and slams shut when that flips. Valves have no muscles: pressure does all the work.

Words you will meet

Systole / diastole
Squeeze / relax. Ventricular systole pushes blood out; diastole lets the ventricle refill.
Mitral and tricuspid valves
The doors between atrium and ventricle (left and right). Thin sails tied down by cords (chordae tendineae) and little muscles (papillary muscles), so they can’t flip back into the atrium.
Aortic and pulmonary valves
The exit doors: three half-moon pockets each. When blood tries to fall back, the pockets fill and press together.
Stroke volume
How much one beat pushes out, about 70 ml (a small cup).
Cardiac output
Stroke volume × beats per minute, about 5 litres a minute: all your blood, every minute.
Pressure (mmHg)
How hard blood pushes. 120/80 means 120 at the top of each beat in the arteries, 80 at the bottom.

Surroundings heat, cold, fever, altitude, posture

The outside world changes the circulation through the same knobs as everything else: vessel radius, venous filling, heart rate and contractility. Change one and read what the body does about it.

Reflexes

On: the carotid sinus and aortic arch correct MAP within seconds through HR, contractility, arteriolar and venous tone. Switch it off to see the direct effect of a change on its own.

Brain (60–140 mmHg), kidneys (80–170) and heart muscle (60–140) keep their flow nearly constant as pressure changes; coronary flow also follows the heart’s O₂ demand (Guyton ch. 17).

Arterioles resistance vessels, R ∝ 1/r⁴

Set the arteriolar radius in each organ bed. The visible arteries in 3D follow the same tone, but most of the resistance sits in vessels too small to model, so open the magnified view to see them.

Veins capacitance vessels

Venous radius sets how much blood the veins hold without stretching (unstressed volume ∝ r²). Narrowing them moves blood toward the heart and raises mean systemic filling pressure.
